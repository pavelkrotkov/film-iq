# B2 live catalog experiment — 2026-10-30

This dated continuation of the [historical sample](README.md) reports a bounded
TMDB experiment for [issue #12](https://github.com/pavelkrotkov/film-iq/issues/12).
A [later source-stratified effort supplement](review-effort-20261030.md) records actual agent-assisted manual-task wall time; human labor remains unmeasured. The historical timing statements below describe this earlier pass.
It does not establish general catalog accuracy, a missing-catalog rate, human
review time or Gate B acceptance. The frozen convenience sample has fourteen
cases: six from the 2010s, six from 2020–2026 and two older retrospectives.
Source editions, production/premiere assertions and local eligibility remain
separate; the original `sample.json` accepted-ID fields remain null.

## Observed requests

The [movie-search endpoint](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/search-movie)
was queried once per sample title, without a year filter, using English responses
and `include_adult=false`. Pagination required fifteen requests for fourteen
queries. All fourteen searches completed their reported candidate sets: seventy
candidate occurrences in total, not seventy independently audited unique works.
The parent selected one plausible candidate per case for a separate detail lookup;
the selection was a review input rather than an automatic acceptance decision.
Fourteen [movie-detail](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/movie-details)
requests appended `credits,external_ids`; all returned HTTP 200. No retries were
made. A later paired-query supplement used six source-specified alternate titles:
six requests, all HTTP 200 and complete, returning six additional candidate
occurrences. The benchmark therefore used **35 requests: 21 search and 14 detail**;
the 76 search candidate occurrences include repeat works across queries.

The detail timing is the sum of the collector's per-request intervals through
JSON validation and selected-field extraction. It is not total study time,
human effort or a provider comparison. A separate successful authentication
probe preceded this benchmark and is excluded from its 35-request denominator.
An earlier sandbox network failure did not yield a provider HTTP response and
must not be counted as an API rejection or catalog absence. No quota headers
were exposed in these observations; the applicable remaining quota is unknown,
not unlimited. Monetary charges and account-specific allowance were not measured.

## Review and interpretation

An independent automated reviewer inspected the fourteen selected detail records
and all seventy original search summaries, producing **12 accepted and 2
ambiguous** evidence decisions. "Accepted" is this limited audit's decision,
not independent human ground truth or a production resolver's automatic decision.
The parent selected the detail candidates; the remaining 56 original search
summaries lack director/detail checks and are not audited negative matches.
The source basis includes earlier automated source-page reading, plus the new
primary alias checks below. The audit does not establish precision. No candidate
was silently removed; the frozen sample's accepted IDs remain null and the
audit decisions are a separate local overlay. Optional direct IMDb verification
was not performed; IMDb identifiers in detail responses are TMDB assertions.

| Source-period stratum | Cases | Accepted by automated review | Ambiguous | Human review minutes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2010–2019 | 6 | 6 | 0 | null |
| 2020–2026 | 6 | 5 | 1 | null |
| Older retrospective works | 2 | 1 | 1 | null |

By primary source frame, Cannes has 8 accepted and 1 ambiguous among 9 cases;
Berlinale 2 accepted among 2; Annecy 1 accepted among 1; Criterion's derivative
retrospective 1 ambiguous among 1; Film at Lincoln Center 1 accepted among 1.
Each case appears once in these source counts despite additional cited sources.
Every stratum has **zero automatic acceptances**, undefined match precision and
unmeasured human effort. The audit has no unknown case statuses, two unresolved
cases (2/14), and zero confirmed-absent findings; zero findings cannot establish
a missing-catalog rate. Human review sample size is zero. Its recorded automated
wall time was 103.617 seconds after initial instruction/source reading; this is
neither human labor nor the total study time.

The six alternate-title queries retrieved the previously reviewed candidate in
5/6 paired cases. The romanized *Lung Boonmee Raluek Chat* query returned zero
results although its English query had returned the reviewed work. This is
conditional paired retrieval on six selected aliases, not recall against a full
catalog, precision or evidence of absence. The other aliases were the source's
transliterated *A Separation*, *Shoplifters*, *Decision to Leave* and *The Water
Murmurs* titles, plus the French *Portrait of a Lady on Fire* title. No automatic
cross-script equivalence is inferred from these results.

The ambiguous cases remain *The Man Who Could Not Remain Silent* (`history-10`),
with unresolved source/catalog date and runtime contexts, and *Jeanne Dielman*
(`history-13`), whose source film-label year and catalog release year differ.
Release dates are not automatically source production years or world premieres;
a different release event or cut is possible but was not established.

For *The Water Murmurs*, the reviewer found a primary
[BIFF director credit and biography](https://biff.kr/eng/html/program/prog_view.asp?QueryStep=2&c_idx=431&idx=91124)
identifying CHEN Jianying (Story) and the 2022 short. Cannes's selection announcement
(`https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/press/press-releases/2022-short-films-and-la-cinef-selections/`)
credits Story Chen for the same romanized/English work; its film record
(`https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/hai-bian-sheng-qi-yi-zuo-xuan-ya/`) credits
Jianying Chen. The [director's studio portfolio](https://www.storychenstudio.com/the-water-murmurs/)
pairs the English and native-script title. These primary credits support the alias;
the newly observed Cannes 14/15-minute disagreement remains in the audit. The
web-reader observations may reflect crawled snapshots and have no direct HTTP
body hash; they are not human attestations.

Prioritize source-linked alias normalization and explicit human review of dated
events and cut/runtime conflicts. Expand festival shorts and translated-title
cohorts before generalizing from these prominently selected works. Actual human
correction effort, independent human precision, broad coverage and a supported
missing-catalog rate remain unmeasured. Issue #12 and Gate B remain open.
